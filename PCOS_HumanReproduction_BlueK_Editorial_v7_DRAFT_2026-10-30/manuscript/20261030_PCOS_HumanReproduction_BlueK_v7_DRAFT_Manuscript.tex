\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{STUDY QUESTION:} How did changing within-age reference-country population shares contribute to the 1990--2023 change in China's age-standardized PCOS-attributed total-infertility rate difference from an 18-country pooled reference?

\noindent\textbf{SUMMARY ANSWER:} In the GBD point estimates, changing reference-country shares accounted for +88.65 per 100,000 females of the +151.54 change in China's total-infertility rate difference (58.5\% of the signed net change).

\noindent\textbf{WHAT IS KNOWN ALREADY:} GBD studies describe PCOS-attributed infertility burden and trends. Fixed age-standardization weights still permit country composition within each reference age group to change.

\noindent\textbf{STUDY DESIGN, SIZE, DURATION:} This exploratory ecological analysis covered China and 18 other sovereign G20 countries, females aged 15--49 in seven age groups, and 34 years, 1990--2023.

\noindent\textbf{PARTICIPANTS/MATERIALS, SETTING, METHODS:} GBD 2023 prevalence counts and matched female populations yielded age-specific rates, which were standardized using fixed weights normalized over ages 15--49. The primary estimand was the 1990--2023 change in China's total PCOS-attributed infertility rate difference from the contemporaneous population-pooled reference. A Kitagawa-type decomposition separated Chinese rates, reference-country rates and within-age country shares. UN WPP 2024 replaced shares only. Alternative benchmarks, country exclusions, age restrictions, endpoints and annual chaining examined the sensitivity of the comparison.

\noindent\textbf{MAIN RESULTS AND THE ROLE OF CHANCE:} China's total rate rose from 333.56 to 605.08 and the reference rate from 606.48 to 726.45 per 100,000. Their difference narrowed from -272.92 to -121.38. The respective rate and composition components were +271.51 - 208.62 + 88.65 = +151.54 (rounded). UN shares gave +86.76 for composition and +150.69 overall. The 2023 secondary-infertility difference was +2.16 with contemporaneous GBD shares, -47.99 with fixed-1990 shares and -127.89 with equal-country weights. Results are derived point estimates; jointly aligned posterior draws were not obtained for interval propagation.

\noindent\textbf{LIMITATIONS, REASONS FOR CAUTION:} Joint posterior uncertainty in component magnitudes and near-zero differences remains unquantified. The 58.5\% value is a signed accounting ratio, not a causal or case-attributable fraction. The selected G20 comparison does not represent the world or a clinically matched population.

\noindent\textbf{WIDER IMPLICATIONS OF THE FINDINGS:} International reproductive-health comparisons should specify the reference population and report absolute rate and composition components alongside net differences. Contemporary and fixed-composition benchmarks answer complementary monitoring questions; a narrowing difference alone does not indicate a reduction in infertility burden.

\section{Introduction}
Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) affects reproductive health and requires care tailored to the individual \citep{pcos}. Population monitoring complements this clinical perspective by describing national trajectories in PCOS-attributed infertility. In the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study, this outcome is a modeled cause-specific infertility impairment expressed in the general female population \citep{gbd}. Interpreting a national trajectory against a pooled international population requires an explicit definition of that reference.

Liu and colleagues quantified GBD 2019 PCOS-attributed infertility prevalence, disability and geographical trends \citep{liu_pcos2024}. Liang and colleagues analysed GBD 2021 burden, trends and the contributions of population growth, ageing and epidemiological change to burden counts \citep{liang_burden2025}; another study examined China and worldwide trajectories \citep{liang_trend2026}. These studies establish the burden context. The present analysis addresses a different estimand: the change in a national-versus-pooled-reference rate difference, allowing country shares to vary within each age group while the age standard remains fixed (Supplementary Table S9). Its contribution is the empirical interpretation of this comparison using an established decomposition, rather than a new decomposition method.

A contemporaneous population pool represents the country mix at each date. A redistribution of population shares toward countries with lower age-specific rates can lower the pooled rate even when country-specific rates are unchanged. Age standardization fixes the age mix, but not this within-age country mix. Consequently, a smaller absolute gap between the national and pooled-reference rates need not reflect a decline in the national rate. We quantified the 1990--2023 change in China's total PCOS-attributed infertility rate difference from the population-pooled reference of 18 other sovereign G20 countries, allocating that change to Chinese rates, reference-country rates and reference-country shares. Secondary questions concern demographic-source choice, infertility subtypes and fixed-1990 or equal-country benchmarks.

\section{Materials and methods}
\subsection{Design, data and outcome}
This ecological secondary analysis used GBD 2023 Results Tool exports obtained on 25 October 2026 \citep{results}. We analysed females aged 15--49 in seven five-year age groups during 1990--2023. The fixed set comprised 19 sovereign G20 countries, with China excluded from the reference. The 18 reference countries were Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Republic of Korea, Mexico, Russian Federation, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Türkiye, United Kingdom and United States of America. The G20 was established in 1999; we applied this country set retrospectively across the study years. The European Union and African Union were excluded as additional units to avoid overlapping country populations \citep{g20}. This fixed country set defines the international benchmark for the analysis; it is neither a sample representative of the world nor a clinically matched control group.

We selected prevalence (measure 5), PCOS (cause 605), total infertility (impairment 198), primary infertility (220) and secondary infertility (221). Endometriosis (607) was retained as a supplementary comparator. The GBD 2023 household-survey framework identifies primary infertility using absence of a livebirth, desire for a child, union duration of at least five years and no current contraception. Its secondary definition requires a livebirth more than five years earlier and the corresponding union, fertility-intention and contraception conditions. The modeling process combines these survey envelopes with sex- and cause-attribution evidence, including clinical sources, and expresses the final rates among all females in each age group \citep{gbd}. Supplementary data distinguish these quantities from clinical 12-month infertility definitions and from PCOS prevalence itself.

For country $c$, age $a$, year $t$ and infertility type $k$, let $N_{c,a,t,k}$ denote modeled infertility cases and $P_{c,a,t}$ the matched GBD female population. Age-specific prevalence per 100,000 is $r_{c,a,t,k}=10^5N_{c,a,t,k}/P_{c,a,t}$. We checked unique complete cells, finite nonnegative values, primary-plus-secondary additivity and agreement with separately exported endpoint Rate values. Input specifications and hashes are retained in the reproducibility archive.

\subsection{Annual contrasts and the primary period change}
Let $\mathcal{R}$ be the set of 18 reference countries; the labels $\mathrm{CHN}$ and $\mathrm{R}$ denote China and the pooled reference. We used the seven published six-decimal age weights reproduced by Chen and colleagues \citep{chen}, normalized them to sum to one over ages 15--49 and held them fixed. Supplementary Methods report the weight vector, its source limitations and the numerical rounding check.
\begin{align}
 s_{c,a,t}&=\frac{P_{c,a,t}}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{R}}P_{j,a,t}}, &
 A_{\mathrm{R},t,k}&=\sum_a w_a\sum_{c\in\mathcal{R}}s_{c,a,t}r_{c,a,t,k},\\
 A_{\mathrm{CHN},t,k}&=\sum_a w_ar_{\mathrm{CHN},a,t,k}, &
 D_{t,k}&=A_{\mathrm{CHN},t,k}-A_{\mathrm{R},t,k}.
\end{align}
Here $w_a$ sums to one, and $s_{c,a,t}$ sums to one over reference countries within each age and year. Pooling within age precedes standardization. The annual rate difference is $D_{t,k}$; its period change is $\Delta D_k(t_0,t_1)=D_{t_1,k}-D_{t_0,k}$. The primary estimand is $\Delta D_{\mathrm{total}}(1990,2023)$ under contemporaneous GBD shares. Subtype and alternative-reference analyses address secondary estimands. All rates are restricted-age standardized rates per 100,000 females.

\subsection{Symmetric decomposition of the period change}
For endpoints $t_0$ and $t_1$, the symmetric product identity of Kitagawa-type accounting gives \citep{kitagawa}:
\begin{align}
 \Delta D_k(t_0,t_1)&=X_k+Q_k+S_k,\\
 X_k&=\sum_a w_a(r_{\mathrm{CHN},a,t_1,k}-r_{\mathrm{CHN},a,t_0,k}),\\
 Q_k&=-\sum_a w_a\sum_{c\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{s_{c,a,t_0}+s_{c,a,t_1}}{2}(r_{c,a,t_1,k}-r_{c,a,t_0,k}),\\
 S_k&=-\sum_a w_a\sum_{c\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{r_{c,a,t_0,k}+r_{c,a,t_1,k}}{2}(s_{c,a,t_1}-s_{c,a,t_0}).
\end{align}
$X_k$, $Q_k$ and $S_k$ allocate the period change to Chinese rates, reference-country rates and reference-country shares, respectively. Each uses the same endpoints, subtype and population-source specification. Averaging the two replacement orders splits the product interaction symmetrically, giving an exact identity before rounding. Positive components raise the signed China-minus-reference difference. For a difference that remains negative at both endpoints, a positive net change indicates a smaller absolute gap.

The identity is additive across ages and types. The share component is zero if shares are unchanged; it also vanishes when the countries' mean endpoint rates are identical within age, because share changes sum to zero. These algebraic properties clarify the role of composition. Its estimated magnitude in the GBD data is reported separately below.

We report absolute components before the ratio $100S_k/\Delta D_k(t_0,t_1)$. Its signed net denominator contains cancellation between components and can approach zero; ratios can consequently exceed 100\% or change sign. This accounting ratio is distinct from a fraction of women, cases or disease causation. The primary- and secondary-infertility contributions add to the total change. Each subtype contribution can itself be separated into rate and country-share components; subtype percentages and composition percentages should therefore not be added to one another.

\subsection{Population sources and benchmark definitions}
UN WPP 2024 supplied historical mid-year female population estimates for the same 4,522 country--age--year cells \citep{wpp2024}. Values in thousands were converted to persons; projections from 2024 onward were excluded. Only the within-age reference shares were replaced. GBD cases continued to use matched GBD population denominators, and the age standard remained fixed.

Current-year shares compare China with the contemporaneous pooled population. Fixed-1990 shares hold the reference's 1990 within-age country mix constant while country rates vary. Equal-country weights compare China with the arithmetic mean of 18 country rates within age. These benchmarks specify different reference populations and therefore different descriptive estimands, rather than competing estimates of one identical target. The contemporary pool is the primary benchmark because the primary question compares China with the reference population represented at each date. The fixed-1990 benchmark instead isolates a comparison under a constant country mix. Its endpoint difference from the contemporary benchmark is not, in general, equal to the symmetric share component, which averages rates at both endpoints (Supplementary Methods).

\subsection{Structural sensitivity and posterior uncertainty}
We examined both population sources, exclusion of each reference country in turn, ages 20--49 with renormalized age weights, 1990 or 2010 start years, and 2021, 2022 or 2023 endpoints. Adjacent-year decompositions were also summed over 1990--2023 to assess sensitivity to the decomposition path. Supplementary Methods provide a separate age-weight rounding check and the endometriosis endpoint analyses. These analyses assess specification and numerical sensitivity, not posterior uncertainty.

The study exports contain point estimates and marginal uncertainty limits, but no posterior draws with verified joint alignment across the inputs needed for the analysis. We therefore report the derived contrasts, components and ratios as descriptive point estimates conditional on the supplied GBD outputs, without propagated uncertainty intervals or significance tests. Marginal limits alone do not determine joint uncertainty, and independent simulation or resampling the selected countries would introduce additional assumptions rather than recover the GBD posterior dependence. A draw-based analysis would require recomputing the complete pooling and decomposition within each verified joint draw, with particular care for ratios whose net-change denominator approaches zero.

\subsection{Computational reproducibility}
The analyses were exploratory and were not preregistered. Python and base R implementations were used to cross-check the core calculations. The source manifest, code and aggregate results are identified by the immutable analytical snapshot \citep{repository}. Supplementary Methods provide the recorded software versions, execution commands, notation map, numerical checks and GATHER reporting map \citep{gather}. Numerical agreement between implementations checks computation; it does not independently validate the modeled disease estimates.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Coverage and PCOS contrasts}
The PCOS analysis comprised 13,566 country--age--year--type records covering 19 countries, seven age groups, 34 years and three infertility categories. These are model-output records, not independent participant observations. Each population source covered 4,522 country--age--year cells. Completeness, subtype additivity, endpoint rate reconstruction and numerical agreement between the R and Python implementations were checked; the corresponding results, including those for the supplementary endometriosis analysis, are reported in Supplementary Methods.

China's PCOS-attributed total infertility standardized rate increased from 333.56 to 605.08 per 100,000, while the population-pooled reference increased from 606.48 to 726.45 (Table 1; Figure 1). The difference changed from -272.92 to -121.38 and the ratio from 0.550 to 0.833. The narrower negative difference therefore accompanied increases in both standardized rates, rather than a fall in the Chinese rate. Subtype-specific trajectories are shown in Figure 2.

\subsection{Rate and reference-composition components}
The 1990--2023 change in the total contrast was
\begin{equation}
 \underbrace{+271.51}_{X}\;\underbrace{-208.62}_{Q}\;\underbrace{+88.65}_{S}
 =\underbrace{+151.54}_{\Delta D}\quad\text{per 100,000},
\end{equation}
with displayed rounding (Table 2; Figure 3). Chinese rate changes and reference-share changes moved the signed difference upward, while reference-country rate changes offset them. The country-share term was 58.5\% of the signed net change; this denominator is smaller because positive and negative terms cancel.

The country-share component was +88.11 ending in 2021 and +86.74 ending in 2022. The annual-chained component was +87.11 (57.5\%). Across 18 country exclusions and restriction to ages 20--49, the GBD 1990--2023 country-share ratio ranged from 39.8\% to 67.9\% (Supplementary data, Table S2 and Figure S3). The share component remained positive across these specifications. Excluding India gave +56.16 per 100,000 (39.8\% of the net change), whereas excluding the Russian Federation gave +110.34 (67.9\%). Each exclusion changes and renormalizes the reference population; these differences are not country-specific causal contributions.

Secondary infertility contributed +105.14 and primary infertility +46.40 to the +151.54 total change. The secondary contribution was 69.4\% of the signed net change. Its country-share component was +56.81, compared with +31.84 for primary infertility. The 69.4\% subtype contribution and the 58.5\% country-share contribution are different partitions of the same total change and should not be added. In the age-specific decomposition, the largest positive total-infertility share components were +23.76 at ages 40--44 and +20.42 at ages 35--39; the component at ages 15--19 was -2.27 (Supplementary Table S3). These are contributions to the standardized comparison, not estimates of individual age-specific infertility risk.

\subsection{Population sources and benchmark definitions}
UN weighting gave a total contrast change of +150.69, decomposed as +271.51 from Chinese rates, -207.59 from reference rates and +86.76 from country shares (57.6\%; Figure 4). The 2023 total difference was -122.62, compared with -121.38 under GBD shares. Mean total-variation distance between the two within-age reference-share vectors was 1.32 percentage points in 2023 and 1.13 over all years. The reference-share components and total changes were similar under the two population sources. Because both specifications retain the same GBD disease rates, this agreement concerns demographic weighting rather than independent disease-rate validation.

Under contemporary GBD shares, the 2023 primary PCOS difference was -123.54 and the secondary difference was +2.16 per 100,000. Holding the GBD country shares at their 1990 values changed the secondary difference to -47.99; equal-country weighting gave -127.89 (Table 3; Figure 5). The corresponding UN values were +2.26, -48.34 and -127.89. The disease rates and age weights are unchanged within each comparison. Thus, the positive secondary-infertility point difference under contemporary shares is benchmark-dependent; its sign alone does not establish a reliable population ranking.

\section{Discussion}
Reference-country composition made a substantial contribution to the narrowing PCOS-attributed infertility contrast between China and the pooled reference. With a fixed age standard, the 1990--2023 signed rate difference increased by +151.54 per 100,000: +271.51 from Chinese rates, -208.62 from reference-country rates and +88.65 from changing reference-country shares. The close +86.76 share component under UN populations supports stability to this demographic-source substitution. Both China's and the reference's total modeled rates rose, so the narrower contrast describes their relative trajectories alongside the changing population mix.

The contribution is an empirical demonstration of how reference construction changes the interpretation of reproductive-health comparisons, complementing geographical burden and trend studies \citep{liu_pcos2024,liang_burden2025,liang_trend2026}. Fixing age weights does not fix the country mix within each age group. An established symmetric decomposition expresses this composition component in the same units as the rate difference and makes it distinguishable from changes in Chinese and reference-country rates. The findings concern the specified country pool and period, rather than a new decomposition theory or a universal percentage contribution.

For monitoring, the benchmark should match the question. Contemporary pooling compares China with the population represented by the 18-country pool at each date; a fixed historical composition supports comparison over time without changes in that country mix; equal-country weighting instead gives each country equal influence. These are complementary descriptive targets, not a hierarchy of correct and incorrect weights. Reporting the contemporary and fixed-composition contrasts together would make benchmark dependence visible, as illustrated by secondary infertility in 2023. Absolute components should accompany any percentages because opposing rate components reduce the signed net denominator. The 39.8\%--67.9\% range across the specified GBD age and country-exclusion scenarios describes sensitivity to those choices, not posterior uncertainty.

Clinical interpretation also depends on the outcome and denominator. In the 2020 Chinese community survey, Yang and colleagues used Rotterdam PCOS ascertainment among married Han women aged 20--49 and reported infertility characteristics among PCOS participants \citep{yang}. The present analysis instead concerns GBD cause-attributed infertility among all females aged 15--49. These definitions and populations serve different purposes (Supplementary Table S11). The clinical guideline provides the diagnostic and care context \citep{pcos}; the aggregate comparison does not identify individual transitions between infertility categories or effects of diagnosis, treatment or service provision.

The principal limitation is unquantified joint posterior uncertainty: the precision of the absolute components, the 58.5\% ratio and near-zero differences remains unknown. Structural sensitivity analyses cannot replace that uncertainty assessment. Additional limits include heterogeneous upstream evidence and modeled cause attribution, possible shared population inputs between GBD and UN, exploratory analytical choices and the purposively selected G20 country set. The published age weights were not independently matched to an official full-precision IHME vector. However, the rounding envelopes reported in Supplementary Table S8 are narrow and address numerical precision only; they do not establish the provenance of the age standard or the validity of the disease estimates. The results describe this G20 comparison, not the world or a matched clinical control population.

In this descriptive analysis, reference-country composition accounted for +88.65 per 100,000 of the +151.54 change in the modeled China-minus-reference difference. The narrower difference coexisted with increasing infertility rates in China and the reference pool. Reporting absolute rate and composition components, together with clearly defined alternative benchmarks, would distinguish these patterns without interpreting benchmark convergence as improved reproductive health.
