Dear Editors of Reproductive Health,

We would like to submit the Research article “Infertility subtype and reference population composition shape China's contrasts with other G20 countries: an ecological analysis of GBD 2023”.

This analysis addresses how international summaries of infertility should be interpreted. Using female estimates for ages 15–49, we distinguish changes in Chinese rates, reference-country rates and reference-country population shares. In the main comparison, 58.5% of the signed narrowing in the PCOS-related infertility difference corresponded to changing reference composition. Primary and secondary infertility also contributed differently; their apparent aggregation concealed a persistent primary-infertility contrast. Country and endpoint analyses qualify these findings, particularly the unstable 2023 endometriosis comparison.

Previous GBD publications already cover these causes, subtype attribution, China/global trends and conventional decomposition. Our incremental contribution is their joint accounting within an explicitly defined comparison population, rather than a claim of a new method or novelty from database updates alone. We believe this interpretation problem is relevant to the journal's interest in population reproductive health and female fertility.

The manuscript provides a Plain English summary, reproducible scripts, machine-readable derived tables and supplementary sensitivity analyses. It reports that aligned uncertainty draws were unavailable, avoids causal and significance claims, discloses post hoc analyses and documents AI assistance.
